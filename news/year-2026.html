2026-10-30 - Our tissue clocks work featured in Der Standard

The Austrian newspaper Der Standard featured our tissue clocks work in its "Forschung Spezial" section, in the article "Was es bedeutet, wenn Herz, Leber und Niere unterschiedlich altern" ("What it means when heart, liver and kidney age differently").

In an interview with Andre, the article explains how we used AI to read the age of 40 tissue types from over 25,000 histology slides, how organs can fall out of sync as we age, and how we are working towards a blood test for organ-specific aging.

"Die meisten Menschen sind synchron, ihre Leber ist genau so alt wie ihr Herz oder ihr Darm. Aber bei manchen Menschen schert ein Organ oder ein Organsystem aus und altert schneller."

"Most people are in sync, their liver is exactly as old as their heart or their gut. But in some people, one organ or organ system breaks rank and ages faster."

Thank you to Sophie Fessl for the interview! Read the full article (in German) on derstandard.at.

2026-10-20 - Andre speaks at the Young Academy Science Day

Andre gave a talk at the Science Day of the Young Academy of the Austrian Academy of Sciences (ÖAW), held on October 20th in the Johannessaal of the Academy in Vienna and dedicated to the many dimensions of time, with researchers from across disciplines reflecting on "time" from their own perspectives.

In the first panel, "Time and the Body", Andre presented "The body keeps time: Reading organ-specific aging from a drop of blood". The talk explored the different kinds of time our bodies keep: the daily circadian clock, seasonal rhythms in our immune system, and aging, the one clock that only moves forward. Andre then presented our tissue clocks work, where we used AI to read biological age from routine microscopy images of healthy tissues, showing that each of our organs keeps time in its own way and that organ aging can be estimated from a blood sample.

Thank you to the organizers and fellow members of the Young Academy for a day of great interdisciplinary exchange!

2026-08-06 - StainX preprint is now available on BioRxiv!

Our manuscript describing StainX is now available as a preprint on BioRxiv.

StainX is a Python package for GPU-accelerated batch stain normalization of histopathology images, achieving 8-11x speedups over standard PyTorch implementations through optimized CUDA kernels.

The manuscript, led by Samir Moustafa, details the algorithm implementations and benchmarking.

Read the preprint at doi:10.64898/2026.08.06.743198, install with pip install stainx, and check out the GitHub repository.
